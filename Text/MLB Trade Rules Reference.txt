MLB TRADE RULES REFERENCE
2026 SYSTEM VALUES
- Governing Agreement: 2022-2026 MLB Collective Bargaining Agreement and applicable Major League Rules
- Competitive Balance Tax Base Threshold: $244 million
- CBT Surcharge Thresholds: $264 million, $284 million, and $304 million
- Major League Minimum Salary: $780,000
- Standard Active Roster: 26 players (maximum 13 pitchers)
- September Active Roster: 28 players (maximum 14 pitchers)
- Reserve (40-Man) Roster: 40 players
- 2026 Trade Deadline: August 3, 2026 at 6:00 p.m. Eastern Time

1. NO SALARY MATCHING REQUIREMENT
MLB generally does not require teams to match incoming and outgoing player salaries.
A club may acquire substantially more or less salary than it sends away.
Validation:
Do not reject a trade because incoming salary exceeds outgoing salary.

2. NO HARD TEAM SALARY CAP
MLB does not impose an NBA-style team salary cap or apron system.
There is no general CBA salary-matching ceiling on a player's acquisition by trade.
The Competitive Balance Tax may create financial consequences rather than prohibit the trade.

3. COMPETITIVE BALANCE TAX
For 2026, the base CBT threshold is $244 million.
Tax payroll generally uses contract average annual values (AAV), plus applicable benefits
and other adjustments rather than just current-year cash salaries.
Basic repeat-offender rates on amounts above the base threshold:
- First consecutive year: 20%
- Second consecutive year: 30%
- Third consecutive year or more: 50%
Crossing a CBT threshold does not automatically invalidate a trade.

4. COMPETITIVE BALANCE TAX SURCHARGES
The 2026 surcharge thresholds are approximately:
- $264 million: base threshold + $20 million
- $284 million: base threshold + $40 million
- $304 million: base threshold + $60 million
Additional surcharge rates apply at progressively higher payroll levels.
At $40 million or more above the base threshold, specified future draft-pick
placement penalties can also apply, subject to the top-six-pick exception.
Validation:
Calculate separately from transaction legality; do not reject solely for CBT impact.

5. 40-MAN ROSTER LIMIT
A Major League club generally cannot exceed 40 players on its 40-man roster.
A player arriving on a 40-man roster contract generally requires an available
40-man roster spot on the acquiring team.
A trade may create a vacancy by sending another 40-man player away.
Validation:
Count each team's post-transaction 40-man roster independently, including required
corresponding roster moves. If there is no legal way to create space, flag the deal.

6. ACTIVE ROSTER LIMIT
From Opening Day through August 31, the normal active roster is 26 players, with a
maximum of 13 designated pitchers; eligible two-way players are treated specially.
From September 1 through the end of the regular season, it is 28 players, with a
maximum of 14 designated pitchers. The postseason roster normally returns to 26.
Validation:
If an acquired player will be active, validate a compliant active roster or identify
an available option, injured-list, or other corresponding transaction.

7. UNEQUAL NUMBERS OF PLAYERS
Teams generally may exchange unequal numbers of players.
Examples include one player for multiple prospects or multiple players for one player.
There is no general rule requiring equal player counts or equal player salaries.
Validation:
Check roster capacity and each player's individual eligibility, not equal counts.

8. CONTRACTUAL NO-TRADE CLAUSES
A player may have a full or partial contractual no-trade clause.
- Full clause: player consent is needed for a trade to another club.
- Partial clause: consent is needed when the destination is a protected club.
The list of protected teams may change under the terms of the contract.
Validation:
Compare the destination against the player's current contractual rights.
Without required consent, mark the deal conditional or prohibited as appropriate.

9. TEN-AND-FIVE TRADE CONSENT RIGHTS
A player with at least 10 years of Major League service, including the past five
consecutive years with the same club, has the right to veto a proposed trade.
These rights apply even if the player lacks a separately negotiated no-trade clause.
Validation:
Require player consent before treating such a trade as executable.

10. RECENTLY SIGNED MAJOR LEAGUE FREE AGENTS
An applicable Article XX(B) free-agent signing generally cannot be assigned before
the following June 16 without the player's written consent under the relevant CBA rule.
The consent exception contains additional conditions, including a restriction on
cash consideration. This rule should not automatically be applied to every contract.
Validation:
Check the type and date of the free-agent signing, applicable consent, and any
conditions on an early assignment.

11. RECENTLY DRAFTED PLAYERS
Players signed following the Rule 4 amateur draft generally cannot be traded until
the day after the World Series ends in their draft year.
An ineligible newly drafted player cannot be used to bypass the restriction merely
by labeling him a player to be named later.
Validation:
Compare draft/signing year and the applicable World Series completion date.

12. PLAYERS UNDER CONTRACT AND FREE AGENTS
An MLB club can trade the contractual rights it actually holds, subject to restrictions.
An unrestricted free agent who has left the club's control is not a player asset
that the former club may include in a trade.
Pre-arbitration and arbitration-eligible players are generally tradeable while
under club control, subject to other applicable restrictions.

13. MINOR LEAGUE PLAYERS NOT ON THE 40-MAN ROSTER
Eligible Minor League players on Minor League contracts may generally be traded.
Players not on a 40-man roster may generally still be traded after the Major League
Trade Deadline, subject to applicable contract and transaction restrictions.
Validation:
Distinguish a Minor League contract from a Major League contract and verify 40-man
status as of the transaction date.

14. MAJOR LEAGUE TRADE DEADLINE
The 2026 MLB Trade Deadline was August 3, 2026 at 6:00 p.m. ET.
After the deadline, clubs cannot trade players on 40-man rosters during the
restricted in-season period. The old August waiver-trade system no longer exists.
Eligible non-40-man Minor Leaguers may still be traded after the deadline.
Validation:
Store the trade deadline as a season-specific variable and check transaction time.

15. OFFSEASON TRADE WINDOW
Normal Major League player trading resumes after the World Series in the applicable
offseason transaction window, subject to league rules and player-contract rights.
The in-season Trade Deadline is not a permanent prohibition on future trades.
Validation:
Use an annual transaction calendar, not a blanket after-deadline ban.

16. PLAYER TO BE NAMED LATER (PTBNL)
A trade may contain a player to be named later, subject to eligibility rules.
The remaining player generally must be identified within six months.
The designated player must be eligible for assignment under the applicable rules.
A PTBNL cannot be used to evade a substantive ban on trading that player.
Validation:
Record the agreement date, resolution deadline, eligibility and eventual identity.

17. CASH CONSIDERATIONS
MLB teams may generally include permitted cash considerations in player trades.
There is no NBA-style general annual cash-trade allowance or second-apron restriction.
Cash terms must still comply with applicable MLB rules, contractual rights and
special restrictions on particular types of transactions.
Validation:
Track payments, dates, payer, recipient and any special transaction limitations.

18. RETAINED SALARY AND FINANCIAL OBLIGATIONS
Clubs may negotiate cash payments and allocation of remaining contract obligations
in trades, subject to MLB rules and player-contract terms.
Salary liability, actual cash paid and CBT payroll accounting are not necessarily
identical measurements.
Validation:
Model each remaining contract year, cash transferred and CBT consequences separately.

19. ORDINARY AMATEUR DRAFT PICKS
MLB clubs generally cannot trade ordinary Rule 4 amateur draft selections.
This includes normal first-round picks and most other regular selections.
Do not apply the NBA's Stepien Rule, future-pick horizon or pick-swap machinery to
these ineligible MLB selections.
Validation:
Reject a draft pick unless it fits a specifically permitted tradeable category.

20. COMPETITIVE BALANCE DRAFT PICKS
Competitive Balance Round A and Round B picks are the principal tradeable MLB
amateur draft selections under the 2022-2026 system.
- Only the club originally awarded the selection can trade it.
- The receiving club cannot trade the selection again.
- The pick cannot be traded solely for cash consideration.
- Applicable timing and transaction requirements must also be met.
Validation:
Check pick type, originating club, previous transfers, trade consideration and date.

21. INTERNATIONAL BONUS POOL AND DRAFT RIGHTS
International signing bonus pool allotments and international signing rights are
not ordinary MLB player contracts or freely interchangeable draft selections.
Their movement depends on the specific signing-system rules in force.
Validation:
Do not automatically treat bonus pool capacity or unsigned-player rights as an
unrestricted tradeable asset. Apply a separately verified rule set when supported.

22. RULE 5 DRAFT PLAYERS
A player selected in the Major League phase of the Rule 5 Draft can be traded.
The acquiring club generally inherits the Rule 5 active-roster and offer-back
requirements until those restrictions are satisfied or the full rights are acquired.
Validation:
Carry Rule 5 status with the player and check the acquiring club's roster plan.

23. INJURED PLAYERS
An injury or placement on an injured list does not by itself make every trade illegal.
The player's medical condition, contract and any applicable transaction restrictions
must be evaluated. Trades may depend on medical review or related conditions.
Validation:
Do not categorically exclude all injured players; flag unresolved medical or
contractual conditions for review.

24. 60-DAY INJURED LIST AND 40-MAN SPACE
A player properly placed on the 60-day injured list does not occupy a 40-man spot
while on that list. A club must restore the player to the 40-man roster when required.
The timing of 60-day IL eligibility and offseason reinstatement matters.
Validation:
Project roster counts after all required activations and corresponding moves.

25. MINOR LEAGUE OPTIONS
A 40-man player may have option years allowing assignment to the Minor Leagues
without first clearing outright waivers.
- Players typically receive three option years, with certain fourth-year cases.
- At most five optional assignments are generally permitted in a season.
- Players with at least five years of Major League service have consent rights for
  optional assignment.
Validation:
Do not treat acquisition as prohibited solely because a player lacks options;
assess whether the receiving club can legally keep him on its active roster.

26. DESIGNATED FOR ASSIGNMENT (DFA)
DFA removes a player from the 40-man roster.
A designated player generally must be traded or placed on the appropriate waivers
within seven days of the designation.
Validation:
If a traded player is currently designated for assignment, account for the DFA
deadline and whether an assignment can be completed lawfully.

27. WAIVERS AND OUTRIGHT ASSIGNMENTS
Waiver claims and trades are legally different transactions.
A post-deadline outright waiver claim is not a negotiated player-for-player trade.
Some players possess consent or free-agency election rights when a club attempts
to assign them outright to the Minor Leagues.
Validation:
Do not model waiver claims as trades or assume every waiver outcome is controllable.

28. FREE-AGENCY AND ARBITRATION CONTROL
A player generally becomes eligible for MLB free agency after accumulating six
years of Major League service, unless a binding contract covers later seasons.
Many players become arbitration-eligible after three years, with some Super Two
players eligible earlier.
Trades do not reset accumulated Major League service time.
Validation:
Project club control and arbitration status using service time and contract terms.

29. CONTRACT OPTIONS, OPT-OUTS AND INCENTIVES
Contracts may contain club options, player options, mutual options, opt-out clauses,
bonuses, deferrals and other provisions affecting future financial obligations.
A trade does not automatically extinguish contractual rights.
Validation:
Carry applicable options and consent provisions with the acquired contract, and
evaluate any specific trade-triggered condition.

30. POSTSEASON ROSTER ELIGIBILITY
A player generally must have been in the organization's system by the end of
August 31 to qualify for postseason eligibility, subject to MLB's roster and
injury-replacement provisions.
A trade legally executed after the Major League deadline does not automatically
make an acquired Minor Leaguer postseason-eligible.
Validation:
Track postseason eligibility separately from whether the underlying trade is legal.

31. MULTI-TEAM TRADE VALIDATION
MLB permits multi-team trades, subject to each club's legal ownership and transfer
of the assets it sends and receives.
Validate every participating club and every player independently.
Important checks include:
- Player is under the sending club's control and otherwise trade-eligible
- Transaction is permitted on the trade date
- Required player trade consent has been obtained
- Each receiving club has or can legally create 40-man and active-roster space
- Any draft selections, PTBNLs and special rights are eligible for transfer
- Rule 5 requirements and contract provisions carry over when applicable
- Cash and contract obligations are validly recorded
- CBT consequences are calculated without inventing a salary-matching prohibition
For incomplete consent, corresponding moves or medical information, distinguish
CONDITIONAL from ILLEGAL rather than automatically treating every uncertainty as a ban.
